Process

  • Identify issue → confirm evidence in HubSpot (date, summary, impact, links) → apply the tag in Moo with rationale.
  • SLA 1/2 → notify the relevant party with a factual summary. Termination → follow Internship Change Guidelines.
  • Previously Blacklisted HC re-engaging → mandatory review call before any new intern assignment.
Host Company Blacklist Process Trigger events, decision flow, system steps

PSA identifies, PSA + PM decide together. Evidence-based, protective of intern safety and program integrity.

Trigger events

  • Safety & legal — harassment, discrimination, unsafe conditions, fraud → skips straight to decision meeting, no intervention phase required.
  • Severe SLA violations — persistent onboarding failures, 3+ missed WFLs with no communication.
  • Unprofessional conduct or negative internship impact across consecutive cycles.

Process

  • Document the incident in HubSpot → flag internally, tag PMs → classify Minor / Major / Critical.
  • Not critical → pushback conversation + POA + 2–4 week monitoring period first.
  • Unresolved → decision meeting (PSA + PM) → Blacklist, Final Warning, or re-engage under stricter terms.
  • Blacklist in Moo with a specific reason code. No proactive notification to the HC.

If a blacklisted HC asks why they've lost dashboard visibility: acknowledge calmly, give factual non-inflammatory reasoning, don't assign blame, document everything in HubSpot. Simone can be consulted for complex cases but isn't required for standard decisions.

Internship Change Guidelines When a change is warranted, RESTART vs RESUME, timelines

A change is not the default solution — only after reasonable efforts at coaching, mediation, and realignment. Interns dismissed from an HC get one replacement only.

Not sufficient on their own

  • "Don't like the work" / find it boring · no daily tasks (interns are expected to be proactive) · timezone mismatch unless truly incompatible · small/early-stage company · cultural difference without disrespect.

Fault determines the new timeline

  • HC fault (misconduct, severe misalignment, prolonged unresponsiveness) — new start date is 2 weeks out, matching begins immediately.
  • Intern fault — must book a call with PSA within 1 week (or withdrawn) and complete a reflective task before the 6-week placement process begins.

RESTART vs RESUME

  • RESTART — full duration reset at the new placement (16 weeks max).
  • RESUME — completed hours carry over, remaining duration adjusted (16 weeks max total).

Before actioning: notify PMs/PRS/PAM in Slack. Intern-fault changes require a call with the intern first. Credit-bearing programs (ASU, BAU) need end-date alignment confirmed explicitly.

Escalation & mediation
Mediation Playbook Fast triage, the mediation spine, when to escalate

A judgment framework, not a script library. Most intern–HC conflict comes from expectation, communication, or skill gaps — not bad intent.

Fast triage — which bucket is this?

  • Performance concern · Expectation mismatch · Communication breakdown · Capacity/structural constraint · Values/fit tension. Many cases span more than one — don't lock in too early.

The mediation spine (never skip a step)

  • 1. Gather information — acknowledge within 48h, speak to each side separately first.
  • 2. Identify the core issue — separate symptoms from root cause.
  • 3. Confirm key facts — anchor in timestamps and evidence, not story-vs-story.
  • 4. Choose the right intervention — skill / expectation / communication fix, lightest viable first.
  • 5. Communicate the decision — always include a 1–2 week observation window, confirm in writing.
  • 6. Follow up and monitor — this is where cases actually succeed or fail.

Escalate when

  • Benchmarks are repeatedly missed · one party disengages · no willingness to adjust · program integrity or safety is at risk. Escalation is a decision, not a failure — don't let a case drag beyond 1–2 weeks.
Decision-Making & Escalation Playbook When to own it vs when to escalate

If it's a risk, escalate. If it's a decision, own it. The default expectation in the PSA role is ownership — discomfort alone isn't a reason to escalate or wait for approval.

Escalation is required for

  • Genuine compliance/university risk · legal exposure · safety concerns · reputational risk.

Escalation is not required for

  • Temporary delays (OOO, holidays) · resolvable ambiguity · anything a pause, task-bank assignment, or timeline adjustment can handle.

Common over-escalation patterns to watch for in yourself

  • Escalating discomfort instead of risk · escalating before checking SOPs/precedent · treating escalation as a pause button · escalating ambiguity that a reasonable assumption would resolve.

Good escalation includes context, what's already been tried, options considered, and a recommendation — it should accelerate progress, not hand off ownership.

Product & Platform Knowledge How VI works, Moo basics, revenue model
  • 18 Career Fields (CFs) — interns work 3 CF-related tasks + 2 cross-functional activities.
  • Revenue: ~90% B2B (universities, pathway providers, bootcamps), ~10% B2C. Host companies never pay — no fees, no salaries required.

  • Moo is the internal ops platform — PSAs live mainly in "During Internship" and its subpages.

  • 1 voicemail left
  • 1 text, no response
  • 1 email, no response

W1Call script

Week 1 — Start Confirmation

When: Every intern, Week 1, by Wednesday EOD.

Unreachable threshold: 2 calls + 1 voicemail + 1 text + 1 email, no response.

Purpose: Confirm the internship actually started — supervisor contact, task clarity, working rhythm. Missing any one of the three → act same day.

Programme requirement, not optional. Unreachable interns → flag PAM immediately. Unresponsive hosts (24h) → escalate, subject to SLA tagging.

Call script

Opening"Hi [Name], this is [PSA name] from Virtual Internships. I'm your Placement Success Advisor — I'm your main point of contact during your internship. I check in with everyone in their first week to make sure the start has actually happened properly. Is now a good time?"
Category 1 — Supervisor connection (mandatory)"Walk me through what's happened since your start date. Have you met your supervisor yet — and how did that go?"
Listen for: clear evidence of contact and interaction, not assumptions or intentions.
If vague: "When did you last speak to them, and what was it about?"
If no meeting yet: "Have you sent them an introduction message? What happened when you did?"
Category 2 — Task clarity (mandatory)"If I asked you to describe your main project or first task in two sentences, what would you say?"
Listen for: concrete tasks or outputs. Risk signal: "just helping out" or "waiting for instructions."
If waiting on supervisor: "Have you let them know you're ready and waiting? What did you say?"
Category 3 — Working rhythm (mandatory)"Talk me through how your 20 hours a week will actually happen — which days, what times, and how will you stay in touch with your supervisor?"
Listen for: a realistic, specific plan — not a general intention.
If plan is vague: "Have you and your supervisor agreed on check-in times, or is that still to be worked out?"
If hours seem at risk: "On a scale of 1–10, how confident are you that you'll hit 20 hours this week — and what would get in the way?"
Admin confirmation"Two important things to confirm: your Weekly Feedback form opens every Thursday and you need to log hours by Friday end of day every week — even if it's a quiet week. Your skills assessments also sit in the same place."
CareerBridge"If you're ever waiting on tasks or your supervisor is slow to respond, don't sit idle. We have CareerBridge resources you can work on in the meantime, and those hours still count."
Expectations"I'll check in with you at key points, and you can reach out between those if anything comes up. If something feels unclear — no tasks, no response from your supervisor, anything like that — flag it early. Most things you'll be able to resolve yourself with some guidance from me. If not, that's when I step in directly."
Close"Before we wrap up — what's the one thing that's still unclear for you right now?" Then confirm: "So your next step is [intern states action]. I'll check in on that. Good luck with your first week."

If resolution requires more than one agreed action, end the call and follow up asynchronously. Do not solve on the call.

W3Call script

Week 3 — Trajectory Check

When: Every intern, Week 3, any day — regardless of WFL scores or engagement status.

Unreachable threshold: 2 calls + 1 voicemail + 1 text + 1 email, no response.

Before dialling: Pull the Week 1 call log. That status is your entry point.

Purpose: Assess trajectory across work output, hours, and host relationship. Log a trajectory projection in the intern's own words.

Programme requirement. Unreachable interns → flag PAM immediately. Unresponsive hosts → subject to SLA tagging.

Call script

Opening"Hi [Name], [PSA name] here. You're three weeks in, which is when I check in properly with everyone - not because anything's wrong, but because this is the point where it's a good time to get a read on what the flow looks like. Is now a good time?"
Category 1 — Work output (mandatory)"Walk me through your last working day, start to finish - what did you actually do?"
Listen for: concrete activity. Vague accounts or "waiting" are output risk signals regardless of hours logged.
If account is thin: "Tell me about something you've worked on so far that you're genuinely proud of, or that you've enjoyed."
If unclear what they're producing: "When you finish a task, what happens to it - does your supervisor review it, use it, give you feedback?"
Category 2 — Hours trajectory (mandatory, every call)"Looking at your hours, you're at [X] against a target of [Y]. Talk me through what your week actually looks like - is that pace sustainable to the end?"
If a gap exists: "What's getting in the way - is it task availability, your schedule, or something with your supervisor?"
If host-side issue: "Your first move is raising it with your host - let's word that message together now. And while that lands, start CareerBridge today so the hours keep counting."
Category 3 — Host relationship (mandatory)"How do you think your supervisor would describe your work so far?"
"I honestly don't know" = limited feedback signal. A confident, specific answer = healthy.
If relationship is unclear: "When did you last get feedback from them - and what did they say?"
To surface avoidance: "Is there a conversation with your host you've been putting off?"
Trajectory projection"Here's the question I ask everyone at this point: if nothing changes between now and the end, how do you think this internship finishes? And what would you want to be different?"
Close"So, in your own words: what's the one thing you're going to do before [specific date]?" Then confirm: "I'm noting that down, and I'll check in on it."

If resolution requires more than one agreed action, end the call and follow up asynchronously. Do not solve on the call.

5ACall script — Track A

Week 5A — Finish Strong

Applies to: 6–8 week programmes entering the final stretch.

When: Any day, Week 5.

Unreachable threshold: 2 calls + 1 voicemail + 1 text + 1 email, no response — treated as Critical immediately.

Before dialling: Pull the Week 3 call log — your entry point, not a historical note. If Week 3 was At Risk or Critical, apply a higher bar: corroborating evidence required before scoring 4 or 5 in any category.

Programme requirement. Unresponsive hosts → subject to SLA tagging.

Call script

Opening"Hi [Name], [PSA name] here. You're in the home straight - about ten working days left - so this call is about making sure you finish strong and nothing trips you up at the end. Is now a good time?"
Category 1 — Hours to target (mandatory)"You've logged [X] hours, you need [Y] more, and you've got [submissions/assessments] outstanding. Walk me through your plan for getting those done in the time left."
If gap is large: "What could realistically get in the way of that plan? And what do we do if it happens?"
If hours cannot be met through host work alone: "CareerBridge can help close part of this - let's choose the resources now so the hours count."
Category 2 — Execution plan (mandatory)"Is there anything still unresolved with your host - unclear final deliverables, handover work, anything you've been putting off - that we should sort this week rather than leaving it to the end?"
If loose ends exist: "What's your first move on that - and when will you make it?"
Category 3 — Intern ownership (mandatory)"Beyond just completing: what do you want your host's final feedback to say about you? What would you need to do in these last two weeks for them to say it?"
If intern is passive: "If your supervisor had to describe your initiative in the last two weeks in one word, what do you think they'd say - and is that what you want?"
Close"So by [Friday date] you'll have logged [n] hours and submitted [items] - that's the plan in your words. I'll be watching the Friday report and will check in if anything slips."

If resolution requires more than one agreed action, end the call and follow up asynchronously. Do not solve on the call.

Week 5B — Midpoint Course-Correct

Applies to: 8+ week programmes at the midpoint.

When: Any day, Week 5.

Unreachable threshold: 2 calls + 1 voicemail + 1 text + 1 email, no response — treated as Critical immediately.

Before dialling: Pull the Week 3 call log. The Week 3 commitment is the first question on this call, not a formality. If Week 3 was At Risk or Critical, apply a higher bar: corroborating evidence required before scoring 4 or 5.

Programme requirement. Unresponsive hosts → subject to SLA tagging.

Call script

Opening"Hi [Name], [PSA name] here. You're roughly halfway through, which is the best point to take stock - enough time behind you to be honest about how it's going, and enough time ahead to change it. Is now a good time?"
Category 1 — Hours to target (mandatory)"You're at [X] hours against [Y] expected by now. At this pace you finish at [projection]. Talk me through it - and let's decide what needs to change this week, not later."
State the projection clearly. Do not soften the number.
If trajectory is off: "Is this a scheduling issue, a task availability issue, or something else? Which of those can you fix yourself this week?"
Category 2 — Follow-through (mandatory)"Last time we spoke you committed to [commitment from Week 3 log]. How did that go?"
Completed: acknowledge clearly. Not completed: "what got in the way?" Do not move past this lightly.
If not completed: "Was it that you couldn't do it, or that you chose not to prioritise it? I'm asking because the answer changes what we do next."
Category 3 — Execution plan (mandatory)"Here's the big one: if the second half of this internship looked exactly like the first half, would you be satisfied at the end? Why, or why not?"
To make it concrete: "So let's design the second half: what's the one change that would make the biggest difference - and what's the first step you'll take before [date]?"
Supervisor relationship check: "Is there a conversation you've been avoiding with your host - about more responsibility, clearer feedback, or different work?"
Evidence check"What's the strongest piece of work you can point to so far? And what do you want to be able to point to by the end that doesn't exist yet?"
Close"I've noted the strongest work you've done so far and what you want to be able to point to by the end — that's your benchmark for the second half. I'm logging your commitment and I'll check in on it. The interns who finish strongly are the ones who make a change at exactly this point."

If resolution requires more than one agreed action, end the call and follow up asynchronously. Do not solve on the call.

Escalation & edge cases

Unreachable protocol

An intern is Unreachable when, within the same week, all of the following have occurred: two call attempts made with no answer · one voicemail left · one text sent with no response · one email sent with no response.

  • Log all contact attempts with timestamps in HubSpot immediately.
  • Flag to PAM — do not wait for end of week.
  • Contact the supervisor to confirm whether the intern is attending and engaging.
  • Apply the Unreachable outcome from the relevant week's rubric.

SLA tagging applies. An intern who remains unresponsive after PAM visibility is raised is subject to SLA tagging per the Reliability & SLA Tagging Framework. This is not discretionary.

Ticket responses

  • SLA: respond within 48 hours. This is the maximum, not the target.
  • Same day: non-attendance, complete communication breakdown, safeguarding concerns.
  • Log all contact and outcomes in HubSpot.
  • Ticket surfaces a risk equivalent to a ≤7 score → treat as a score-triggered follow-up.
  • Ticket from an intern implicates the supervisor → contact the supervisor the same week.

The aim is not to keep every internship running at all costs. The aim is to identify issues early, have the right conversations, and make the right decision.

Role operating principle

For onboarding and deeper role understanding. If you're mid-caseload, you want Daily Reference or Call Scripts, not this page.

What does a PSA do?

PSAs manage the during-internship experience: running structured check-in calls, monitoring weekly feedback scores, identifying risks, ensuring smooth communication, and building trusted relationships with both interns and host companies.

How the team fits together: Pre-Internship (PMs/PRS) match interns with host companies and prepare interns for placement — comparable to Talent Acquisition. During Internship (PSAs) support interns and HCs throughout the internship itself — comparable to HR/People Operations.

PSAs sit in the messy middle — constantly balancing three simultaneous pulls, rarely with perfect clarity handed to them. Navigation, trade-offs, and relationship management under pressure are core to the role.

Vision, mission & values

Vision: We empower interns and host companies to succeed by turning every challenge into an opportunity for growth, connection, and measurable impact.

Mission: To protect and improve the remote internship experience by ensuring every intern and host company has the clarity, support, and consistency needed for success.

  • Resourcefulness: We navigate incomplete info and messy situations with persistence and creativity.
  • Candor with care: We speak openly and honestly, balancing clarity with respect.
  • Progress over perfection: Small wins compound into lasting impact, even when conditions aren't perfect.
  • Teamwork across time zones: Our strength is in supporting each other, always.

What great looks like in practice:

  • Ask why at least twice before acting
  • Share a clear action plan and check back to confirm improvement, not just resolution
  • Frame issues as development opportunities — coach, don't just fix
  • Maintain trust during difficult conversations; leave both sides feeling heard

PSA craft & expertise areas

Five core craft elements:

  • Clarity: Set everyone up by ensuring interns and HCs fully understand expectations from day one.
  • Support: Consistent guidance and problem-solving so interns grow and HCs feel confident.
  • Monitoring: Track weekly feedback and engagement signals to spot risks early.
  • Escalation & Mediation: Use structured frameworks and judgment to resolve challenges while protecting relationships.
  • Mentorship Facilitation: Enable meaningful mentorship by coaching HCs on light-touch ways to develop interns.

Three expertise domains:

  • Remote work: Help interns adapt to HC workflows, build tool confidence, and bridge cultural gaps.
  • Empowering interns: Create structure for accountability; intervene only after interns have tried to solve challenges themselves.
  • Supporting managers: Act as an HR/People advisor: feedback support, expectation setting, friction reduction.
