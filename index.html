SOP: Host Company Blacklisting Trigger events, decision flow, system steps

Purpose: evidence-based, PSA + PM process for blacklisting HCs whose actions risk interns or VI.

Roles

  • PSA: monitors WFLs/feedback, documents incidents, initiates pushback, drafts POAs, prepares escalation reports.
  • PM: reviews evidence with PSA. Co-decides: blacklist / final warning / re-engage under stricter terms.

1. Trigger events

  • Safety & legal: harassment, discrimination, unsafe conditions, fraud, illegal activity, breach of contract.
  • Severe SLA violations: persistent onboarding failure despite intervention; 3+ missed WFLs with no communication; refusal to complete evaluations after multiple reminders.
  • Unprofessional conduct: rude, abusive, or inappropriate communication toward interns or VI staff.
  • Negative internship impact: multiple interns report no meaningful tasks, unethical treatment, or poor mentorship across consecutive cycles.

2. Initial assessment (PSA)

  • Document the incident in HubSpot with dates, descriptions, evidence.
  • Post and pin a summary tagging the relevant PMs.
  • Classify severity: Minor / Major / Critical. Critical cases skip straight to the decision meeting.

3. Intervention & pushback phase

Required before blacklisting (skip if Critical): HC conversation → POA with agreed steps and deadlines → 2–4 week monitoring. Resolved → close, flag HC "At Risk – Monitored." Unresolved → decision meeting.

4. Decision meeting (PSA + PM)

Prepare a case summary (timeline, evidence, prior attempts) → review collectively → decide Blacklist / Final Warning / Re-engage under stricter terms → log the decision in Moo and HubSpot.

5. System updates (PSA)

  • HC profile in Moo → "Blacklist" → ignore any active-process warning and proceed.
  • Choose the correct reason code (competitor, poor feedback, unresponsive, disrespectful, off-platform activity, etc.) or "Other" with free-text notes (date, reason, decision summary, key attachments).
  • Submit.

6. Post-blacklist follow-up

  • Currently-placed interns → transition or monitor closely to completion.
  • Review all blacklisted HCs every 6 months for continued appropriateness.

Notification

No proactive notification — the HC loses dashboard visibility automatically. Only respond if they reach out.

If the HC reaches out

  • Neutral, factual tone — no blame, no inflammatory language.
  • Focus on program standards and intern safety.
  • PSA + PM align internally on future participation; all decisions in HubSpot.
  • Re-engagement approved → supervised trial → on success, remove blacklist status and monitor via SLA tagging.

Simone can be consulted for complex or high-stakes cases but isn't required for standard decisions.

SOP: Logging WhatsApp Messages What to log, how, and how fast

Log anything about internship progress, scheduling, feedback, key decisions, or escalations. Skip casual small talk unless directly relevant.

Steps

  • Open the intern/supervisor's contact record.
  • More (⋮) → "Log a WhatsApp Message."
  • Copy the message text or screenshot it, paste/upload into the log field.
  • Add a short, clear context summary (e.g. "Intern requested a 1-week deadline extension due to exams").
  • Tag relevant team members if follow-up is needed.
  • Save, confirm association with the correct intern/supervisor/company record.

SOP: Adding Pause Days Confirm return date, check HC, verify against partner limits

Use when: an intern needs time off (illness, exams, personal emergency) or the HC is temporarily unavailable (office closure, supervisor leave).

Before pausing

  • Confirm a specific return date with the intern — not a vague estimate.
  • Confirm with the HC they're aware of the pause dates and still want the intern back. Don't action until both sides confirm.
  • Check the Partner Requirements Cheat Sheet — pause days push the end date out, which may breach partner limits (academic calendar, CPT/visa, batch, 16-week max). The platform won't block this; the check is on you.
  • PAM involvement: a pause is a change to internship parameters, same category as an HC Change or duration change — follow whatever the cheat sheet says for that specific partner rather than a blanket rule.

Adding pause days on the platform

  • Manage Internships → find the intern → same action panel as HC Change → Add pause day(s).
  • Select the specific calendar day(s) being paused (not a day count) — the modal shows the running pause duration as you select.
  • The end date auto-shifts by the number of days selected, shown live in the modal. You can override this and keep the original end date instead if that's the correct call for the case — check the proposed new end date carefully before confirming either way.
  • Enter a reason in the free-text field. No fixed category is required, but be specific enough that anyone reading it later understands what happened — e.g. "Intern hospitalised, returning 22 Aug — confirmed with HC Katherine Tuominen" rather than "personal reasons."
  • Always land the return date on a Monday. When choosing pause days, select however many are needed so the intern's restart date falls on a Monday — this keeps them aligned with the standard WFL week (Thursday open, Friday hours) rather than restarting mid-week.
  • Confirm.

After pausing

  • No manual reminder needed. Because the restart always lands on a Monday, both the intern and the Host Company automatically receive a reminder email the Thursday before, letting them know the internship resumes that Monday. You don't need to track the return date yourself.

Playbooks

Judgment-heavy frameworks for weighing evidence, managing relationships, and decision-making.

Playbook: Mediation Fast triage, the mediation spine, worked examples

A judgment framework, not a script library. Two PSAs following it properly should reach similar decisions regardless of communication style.

Core principle

Most conflict stems from expectation, communication, or skill gaps — not bad intent. Structured process over instinct. Documented, consistent decisions prevent recurrence.

Sequence when a case lands

Pause (don't respond on instinct) → Triage the case → Run the mediation spine, every step → Choose the right intervention, not the fastest → Document continuously.

Fast triage — common case types

  • Performance concern — often hides unclear success criteria, skill–task mismatch, or thin feedback cadence.
  • Expectation mismatch — often hides weak onboarding, ambiguous scope, different seniority assumptions.
  • Communication breakdown — often hides async/sync norm mismatch, timezone friction, early-career habits.
  • Capacity/structural constraint — often hides internal HC workload issues, poor project planning, host over-commitment.
  • Values/fit tension — often hides stress or burnout, bias, mismatched working styles.

The mediation spine

  • 1. Gather information — acknowledge within 48h, calm/neutral/curious tone, speak to each side separately first. Never mediate on assumptions.
  • 2. Identify the core issue — list symptoms, map to skill/expectation/communication/structural causes, check for hidden causes (onboarding gaps, unclear benchmarks), validate against facts. Most poor outcomes trace to weak diagnosis, not bad intent.
  • 3. Confirm key facts — meeting timestamps, tasks assigned vs delivered, response times, feedback already shared. Screenshots help. Prevents story-vs-story deadlocks.
  • 4. Choose the right intervention — balance intern development, HC needs, and program integrity. Offer options not ultimatums, anchor in facts, set an observation window, document decisions.
  • 5. Communicate the decision — always include a 1–2 week observation window, clear actions for both sides, what success looks like, when follow-up happens. Summarise verbally, confirm in writing.
  • 6. Follow up and monitor — track patterns not single slips, progress against benchmarks, early repeat-issue signs. Not optional — this is where cases succeed or fail.

Intervention menu

  • Skill-related: SMART benchmarks, smaller milestones, learning resources/templates/coaching.
  • Expectation-related: reset scope and priorities, define "good" concretely, adjust timelines to match capacity.
  • Communication-related: daily/weekly check-ins, async status updates, explicit response-time norms.

Escalate when

Benchmarks are repeatedly missed · one party disengages · no willingness to adjust · program integrity or safety is at risk. Escalation is a decision, not a failure — don't let a case drag beyond 1–2 weeks. Options: final improvement window, reassignment, early closure.

When both sides are "right"

Reframe: what does the intern need to learn? What does the HC need to protect (time, quality, credibility)? What does the program need to uphold (fairness, consistency, outcomes)? If all three can't be satisfied simultaneously, program integrity outranks individual preference.

Worked examples

  • Performance vs expectation: HC says intern is slow → no onboarding docs, scope misunderstood → reset expectations + checklist + milestones → intern met deadlines, HC satisfied.
  • Communication breakdown: intern misses calls, HC frustrated → timezone misalignment + unclear async norms → weekly check-in schedule + Slack response norms → communication stabilized.
  • Capacity disguised as poor performance: HC says intern "does nothing" → HC overloaded, minimal tasks assigned → redistribute tasks + HC coaching → tasks completed, HC aware of responsibility.
  • Values/fit tension: tone complaints between HC and intern → cultural misunderstanding + early-career directness → 1:1 coaching on norms + reflection call → mutual understanding, de-escalated.
  • Repeated missed benchmarks: intern keeps missing milestones → lacks skills + unclear benchmarks → SMART goals + 1-week observation + escalation window → HC sees progress, structured escalation in place if pattern continues.
Playbook: Decision-Making & Escalation When to own it vs when to escalate

If it's a risk, escalate. If it's a decision, own it. Operating framework for thinking under uncertainty — goal is momentum and clarity, not perfect decisions.

Default expectation: ownership

Uncertainty alone is not a reason to escalate. If progress can be made safely, make it. Use SOPs and precedent; stay accountable for outcomes.

How to think before deciding

  • Identify the real problem: facts vs assumptions, actual blockers vs discomfort, temporary friction vs genuine risk. If nothing is truly blocked, it likely doesn't need escalation.
  • Ground yourself in existing tools: SOPs, the task bank, timeline levers (pauses, extensions, resequencing). These are starting points, not rigid rules — no perfect SOP fit is expected for every case.

Making the call

Own it when: logical given available info · defensible if questioned · reversible if new info emerges. Confidence builds through practice, not pre-approval.

Communicate the decision

State what's happening, what's being done, and what happens next — to interns, HCs, and internal stakeholders.

Escalation is required for

Genuine compliance/university risk · legal exposure · safety concerns · reputational risk to the programme or company.

Escalation is not required for

Temporary delays (OOO, holidays) · resolvable ambiguity · anything a pause, task-bank assignment, or timeline adjustment can handle. These are exactly the scenarios where PSA judgment should be exercised.

How to escalate well

Good escalation accelerates progress, it doesn't pause it: clear context and background, what's already been tried, options considered, a recommended path forward. Work continues in parallel — escalation is not a hand-off of ownership.

Decision-making as a learning loop

Best call with available info → revisit when new info emerges → sense-check after action, not before. Mistakes handled thoughtfully are growth, not failure.

Common over-escalation patterns

  • Escalating discomfort, not risk — "I'm not 100% sure, so I wanted to check." Fix: make a reasonable call, document rationale, adjust if needed.
  • Escalating before using available tools — asking for guidance without checking SOPs or the task bank. Fix: use tools as a starting point even if the fit isn't perfect.
  • Treating escalation as a pause button — waiting to hear back instead of continuing the case. Fix: keep working in parallel, escalate with options and a recommendation.
  • Escalating resolvable ambiguity — asking others to interpret instead of clarifying. Fix: make a reasonable assumption, communicate it, set a check-in point.
  • Using escalation to transfer responsibility — handing off the decision entirely. Fix: escalate with context, options, and a recommendation — stay accountable.

What "good" looks like

  • Temporary delay: supervisor OOO 2 weeks, intern anxious → assign task-bank work, communicate clearly, keep monitoring without escalating.
  • Ambiguity: loosely defined scope → separate known from missing, use SOP intent/precedent for a reasonable assumption, propose a short-term plan with a check-in.
  • Post-decision calibration: paused an internship, new context emerges → explain original reasoning, share the update, adjust the plan — treat it as calibration, not error.

Guide: Reliability & SLA Tagging HC reliability tags — when and how to apply them

Purpose: standardize HC reliability tagging using documented evidence. System: Moo. Evidence sources: HubSpot, WFLs, evaluations, emails, Slack, placement records.

Tags

TagIndicatorOperational action
ReliableConsistently responsive, delivers as promisedNo restrictions; eligible for multiple placements
SLA 1 — Moderate RiskIsolated or minor inconsistency; communication gaps; limited impact on intern experienceHost continues with regular monitoring. Log evidence and follow-up timeline in HubSpot.
SLA 2 — High RiskRecurring or severe issues; pattern of unresponsiveness; significant impact on intern experiencePlacement pause until intervention call resolved. Blacklist before call if warranted. Detailed HubSpot note + link to intervention summary. PAM visibility required before any new intern.

Classification rules

  • Assess frequency, severity, and impact — a single incident does not constitute a pattern.
  • SLA 1: minor or isolated issue with limited impact. SLA 2: recurring or severe issue with significant impact.
  • Tags reflect current reliability, not a permanent label. Reassess when circumstances change.

Tagging process

  1. Review evidence — WFLs, evaluations, communication history, placement records.
  2. Identify pattern — determine frequency, severity, and intern impact.
  3. Apply tag in Moo.
  4. Document in HubSpot — date, factual issue description, impact, supporting links.
  5. Review — reassess when circumstances change or at programme end.

Documentation standard

Every SLA 1 or SLA 2 note must include: date · factual issue description · impact on intern · supporting links (WFLs, emails, Slack). No opinions — facts only.

Guide: Internship Changes When a change is warranted, RESTART vs RESUME, timelines

Changes come after coaching, mediation, and realignment — not as a first response. Interns dismissed from an HC get one replacement only, at the team's discretion.

General rules

  • CF/role changes during an HC change need PRS sign-off and suitable skills.
  • Hours or application-field adjustments need PMS approval before the change, within batch, discussed with PAM.
  • Each placement is a separate internship — max 16 weeks per placement. Prior placement time doesn't count toward the new duration.

Not grounds for a change

  • Don't like the work / bored · no daily tasks (interns must be proactive) · timezone mismatch unless truly incompatible · small/early-stage company · communication or cultural differences without disrespect.

Request categorization

Reason typeImmediate change?Path
Disrespect / misconductYesEscalate to PMS immediately, log in Moo. No mediation — misconduct overrides it. Change immediately; blacklist HC if confirmed.
Severe misalignmentNoConfirm tasks vs offer letter → attempt realignment, 7-day review → change if realignment fails.
Unresponsiveness (10+ days)NoExecute SLA outreach → final 2-day grace period → change + flag if no reply.
Mutual disengagementNoIntake reasons, probe deeper → mediation call within 3 days → change if unresolved.
Intern discontentNoCoach on proactive behavior, discuss alternate projects with HC. No change unless it escalates.
Host discontentNoSupport intern with coaching, 7–10 day review. If persists → possible withdrawal, not change.
Timezone / culture mismatchNoProvide async tools and structured calls, 7–10 day review. Change only if truly unworkable.
Company size / structureNoSet realistic expectations, monitor only. Grounds for change only if tied to misconduct or misalignment.

Process

  • 1. Intake: log in HubSpot, acknowledge within 1 business day, collect detailed reasons from both parties, check background (PMS/PRS notes, SLA tags, offer vs actual tasks).
  • 2. Categorize using the table above.
  • 3. Investigate: clarifying questions, look for miscommunication/unmet expectations/coaching opportunities.
  • 4. Mediate: coaching call, 1-week action plan with clear expectations, reassess at the end.
  • 5. Final decision: resolved → continue with support plan. Unresolved → change, with fault determining the new timeline.

Timeline by fault

  • HC fault (misconduct, severe misalignment, prolonged unresponsiveness): new start date 2 weeks out, matching begins immediately.
  • Intern fault: must book a call with PSA within 1 week or be withdrawn. Call clarifies requirements and expectations. Must then complete a reflective task — approval starts the 6-week placement process. Miss the task deadline → withdrawn.

RESTART vs RESUME

  • RESTART: full duration reset (e.g. 12-week internship → full 12 weeks again). New start/end dates. 16 weeks max.
  • RESUME: completed hours carry over, duration adjusted (e.g. 12-week with 5 done → 7 weeks at new placement). 16 weeks max.

Initiating an HC change (PSA steps)

  • Notify PMs/PRS/PAM in Slack immediately, with intern name, HC name, reason, HubSpot ticket link.
  • Mandatory call with the intern before actioning (skip only if HC is at fault) — confirm the change, outline next steps, answer questions.
  • Clarify with intern: reason, RESTART or RESUME preference, end-date adjustments, batch requirements (academic credit), profile updates.
  • Action in Moo: Manage Internships → search intern → View Internship → Change Company → choose Resume/Restart with reason. This terminates Internship 1 and sets the intern unplaced for Internship 2. Ensure the new start date is ≥2 weeks out for Resume. Update HubSpot notes, add HC Change Tag.
  • Confirm details with intern, schedule PRS call if CF change requested, announce in the HC-Changes-ISA-PRS-PMS Slack channel.
  • Credit-bearing programs (ASU, BAU): the platform auto-updates the end date — verify it aligns with program requirements, confirm hours/weeks needed for on-time graduation, add to notes and the Slack channel, and update the end date once the new placement is confirmed.

Important notes

  • PSAs/PMs must connect with host supervisors on LinkedIn at the start of every internship.
  • Before declaring anyone "unresponsive": at least 3 emails and 1 alternative channel (LinkedIn, WhatsApp, call) attempted.
  • Collaborate closely with PMS when an HC claims skill mismatch, when either side reports no relevant tasks, or when an intern was accepted despite a weaker profile.

  • Reopening a "done" task for new feedback → reset the due date.

Pipelines

General (default) · PMS (Placement Matching) · PRS (Placement Readiness) · ISA (during-internship team) · EDP (Product/Engineering)

  • Resolved — category confirmed, resolution set to No Monitoring or Needs Monitoring before closing.

Creating an outbound ticket

You must create a ticket before emailing an intern (e.g. interview outcome follow-up): HelpDesk → "+" New Ticket → Ticket Name = email subject, Pipeline = ISA, Category, Ticket Owner = yourself, Associate with Contact (intern) and Company → Create. HelpDesk daily practice

  • Daily: review New and Waiting on Us.
  • Every 2 days: review Waiting on Customer / Waiting on Internal Team.
  • Close within 48–72h once scope is confirmed and all queries addressed. A quiet "thank you" thread is safe to close.

Ticket assignment logic

Interns: assigned by type (B2B/B2C) and Intern Learner Status (Application stages → pre-enrollment team; Enrolled & Awaiting Placement → PRS; In Interview/In Offer → PRS or PMS depending on B2B/B2C; Placed onward → ISA). HCs: assigned by company owner.

Technical questions

PM reassigns to the designated technical owner, who takes full ownership. SLA: acknowledge within 1 business day, resolve within 5 business days.

Guide: Product & Platform Knowledge How VI works, Moo basics, revenue model

Product

  • 18 Career Fields (CFs) — interns work 3 CF-related tasks + 2 cross-functional activities. Guaranteed first-choice CF, not guaranteed location or industry.
  • Linear interview process (one company at a time), with urgency-based exceptions near a start date.
  • Durations: 1/2/3/4 months at 10/20/30 hrs/week. Every intern gets an end point coaching call, 24/7 CareerBridge, and monthly webinars regardless of track. All internships are unpaid — any payment is at the HC's discretion.
  • Revenue: ~90% B2B (universities, pathway providers, MOOCs/bootcamps, government-affiliated programs), ~10% B2C. Host companies never pay — zero fees, zero salaries.

HC eligibility criteria

  • Any industry except gambling/casinos, religious institutions, wines & spirits, tobacco.
  • Any geography except a defined restricted-country list (sanctions-driven).
  • Minimum 3 full-time employees, and a live website with a valid URL.
  • Supervisor must be full-time, intermediate+ English, register with a company-domain email. One persona can manage up to 3 interns at a time.
  • 1–2 employee companies: case-by-case approval by Head of Pre-Internship, considering hard-to-place fields and full-time contractor presence.

Moo — the operations platform

The "back-end" of the customer-facing platform. As a PSA you live mainly in "During Internship" and its subpages, occasionally in Registration and Matching. Access arrives via invitation early in week two — complete your profile with name, country, phone number, timezone.

Guide: Curriculum Refresh 2.0 What's changing and why

Replacing the current curriculum: clunky, text-heavy, six years out of date, running on an old LMS, reliant on non-proprietary content.

What's changing

  • 5 courses, each covering a key career-readiness area, 2.5–3.5h each (12–15h total experience).

How to contribute
