🏷 HC Reliability Tagging System: Moo
Guiding Principle
Tag what you can evidence. One incident is not a pattern.
📂
Evidence sources: HubSpot notes · WFLs · evaluations · emails · Slack · placement records

Tags

TagDefinitionIndicators
Reliable Consistently meets hosting expectations. Responsive; provides appropriate work and onboarding; completes required WFLs/evaluations; reliable supervision; no recurring HC issues.
SLA 1 Some inconsistency or reliability concerns without a sustained or significant impact. Occasional late responses; isolated WFL/evaluation gaps; minor onboarding or task issues; communication gaps that are resolved.
SLA 2 Recurring, persistent, or significant reliability issues affecting the placement or intern experience. Repeated missed actions; persistent unresponsiveness; recurring WFL/evaluation gaps; insufficient work or supervision; repeated placement issues; significant mismatch between agreed and actual arrangements.

Classification Rules

  • Assess frequency, severity, and impact — not incident count alone.
  • Reliable: Expectations consistently met; no meaningful pattern of issues.
  • SLA 1: Issues are limited or intermittent; do not materially affect the placement.
  • SLA 2: Issues are recurring, persistent, or materially affect the placement or intern experience.
  • A single isolated incident does not automatically determine the tag.

Tagging Process

  1. Review evidence — communication, WFLs, evaluations, onboarding, tasks, supervision, and placement history.
  2. Identify pattern — assess frequency, severity, and impact.
  3. Apply tag — select the tag that best reflects current HC reliability in Moo.
  4. Document evidence — add factual HubSpot notes when the tag is based on a specific issue or change.
  5. Review when circumstances change — update the tag when new evidence materially changes the HC's reliability profile.

Documentation Standard

Each HubSpot note must include: date · factual issue or evidence · impact (where relevant) · supporting links or records.

ℹ️
Tags represent current HC reliability — not a permanent historical label. Update when evidence changes.
👤 Role Operating Principle Reference · not daily
Guiding Principle
PSAs don't check in. They change trajectories.
What does a PSA do?

PSAs manage the during-internship experience: structured check-in calls, WFL monitoring, risk identification, and trusted relationships with both interns and host companies.

Pre-Internship (PMs / PRS): Match and prepare interns — comparable to Talent Acquisition.
During Internship (PSAs): Support interns and HCs throughout — comparable to HR / People Operations.

PSAs sit in the messy middle, constantly balancing three pulls with rarely perfect clarity. Navigation, trade-offs, and relationship management under pressure are core to the role.

Mindset: Average → Good → Great
AverageGoodGreat
FocusClosing tasksResolving issuesOutcomes & relationships
QuestionWhat's the fastest fix?What's causing this?How do we turn this into progress?
ApproachResponds when prompted · escalates early · applies rules without contextSeeks context · uses SOPs effectively · resolves one-by-oneFinds root causes · communicates with intent · follows through until outcomes improve

Great in practice: Ask why at least twice before acting. Share a clear action plan and confirm improvement, not just resolution. Coach, don't just fix. Leave both sides feeling heard.

Values
  • Resourcefulness – Navigate incomplete info and messy situations with persistence.
  • Candor with care – Speak openly and honestly, balancing clarity with respect.
  • Progress over perfection – Small wins compound, even when conditions aren't perfect.
  • Teamwork across time zones – Strength is in supporting each other, always.
Craft & expertise areas

Five core craft elements: Clarity · Support · Monitoring · Escalation & Mediation · Mentorship Facilitation

Three expertise domains:

  • Remote work – Adapt interns to HC workflows, build tool confidence, bridge cultural gaps.
  • Empowering interns – Create accountability structure; intervene only after interns have tried themselves.
  • Supporting managers – Act as HR/People advisor: feedback support, expectation setting, friction reduction.

🟣 Week 1: Start Confirmation Proactive · Every intern · By Wednesday
Guiding Principle
Three things must exist before Week 1 closes: a supervisor, a task, and a schedule.
🟣
Trigger: Proactive — every intern. Must happen by Wednesday. Make 2 call attempts, leave a voicemail, send a text and email before declaring Unreachable. Non-negotiable programme requirement — flag unreachable interns to PAM immediately.

Call Script

Opening
"Hi [Name], this is [PSA name] from Virtual Internships. I'm your Placement Success Advisor — your main point of contact during your internship. I check in with everyone in their first week to make sure the start has actually happened properly. Is now a good time?"

Category 1 — Supervisor Connection mandatory

"Walk me through what's happened since your start date. Have you met your supervisor yet — and how did that go?"
Listen for: clear evidence of contact and interaction, not assumptions or intentions.
→ Vague: "When did you last speak to them, and what was it about?"
→ No meeting yet: "Have you sent them an introduction message? What happened when you did?"

Category 2 — Task Clarity mandatory

"If I asked you to describe your main project or first task in two sentences, what would you say?"
Risk signal: "just helping out" or "waiting for instructions."
→ Waiting: "Have you let them know you're ready and waiting? What did you say?"

Category 3 — Working Rhythm mandatory

"Talk me through how your 20 hours a week will actually happen — which days, what times, and how will you stay in touch with your supervisor?"
Listen for: a realistic, specific plan — not a general intention.
→ Vague plan: "Have you and your supervisor agreed on check-in times, or is that still to be worked out?"
→ Hours at risk: "On a scale of 1–10, how confident are you that you'll hit 20 hours this week — and what would get in the way?"
Admin Confirmation
"Two important things: your Weekly Feedback form opens every Thursday and you need to log hours by Friday EOD every week — even if it's a quiet week. Your skills assessments sit in the same place."
CareerBridge
"If you're ever waiting on tasks or your supervisor is slow to respond, don't sit idle. We have CareerBridge resources you can work on — those hours still count."
Close
"That's what I needed. I'll be watching the Friday report — if anything needs attention I'll come back to you, otherwise I'll check in again at Week 3."
⚠️ If resolution requires more than one agreed action, end the call and follow up asynchronously. Do not solve on the call.

🟠 WFL Score Review: Weeks 2, 4, 6+ Score ≤7 trigger
Guiding Principle
A low score is always the priority signal. Strong engagement data doesn't cancel it.
🟠
Trigger: WFL score ≤7 from either intern or supervisor in Weeks 2, 4, or any week after Week 5. Also triggered by Disengaged or Slipping engagement status. Primary method: email. Use call/text when situation warrants. Act on a supervisor score immediately — do not wait for the intern's WFL.

How to Read a WFL Score

Before acting, place the score in context:

  • What week is this? A score of 6 in Week 2 means something broke against a known baseline. Week 7 means re-entering after stepping back.
  • Is this intern or supervisor? Do not conflate them. Each side assessed independently.
  • Is this a first low score or a pattern? Single low score with no prior concern ≠ second low after a weak Week 3 status.
  • Does engagement data align? Hours and submissions healthy but score low = outlier. Both weak = signal.

Intern Score ≤7

Step 1 — Review context

Check HubSpot: prior call statuses, engagement status, hours trends, any open flags.

Step 2 — Email intern (Mon/Tue)

"Hi [Name], I've seen your feedback for this week and wanted to check in. I just want to understand how things are actually going — can you walk me through what this week looked like?"

Choose 2–3 questions:

  1. What specifically made you rate it the way you did?
  2. Is this about the work itself, your relationship with your supervisor, or something else?
  3. Has anything changed since we last spoke?
  4. What would need to be different for next week to feel better?
  5. Is there a conversation with your supervisor you've been putting off?

No response within 48h → follow up once more. Still no response → apply SLA process.

Step 3 — Determine root cause

Personal stress, competing commitments, motivation dipIntern-sideCoach the intern. Agree one specific action with deadline. Log commitment verbatim.
Confusion about tasks, unclear expectationsClarity gapHelp intern articulate the question and coach them to raise it with supervisor. If supervisor unresponsive → contact same week.

Step 4 — Week-specific calibration

  • Week 2: You have Week 1 context. Go back to the three foundations — identify which has broken down.
  • Week 4: You have Weeks 1 and 3 context. If intern was At Risk/Drifting at Week 3 and Week 4 score is low, treat as a pattern. Contact host directly.
  • Week 6+: Re-entering after structured phase. Check engagement signals alongside the score. Both weak → treat as Disengaged, not just a low score.

Supervisor Score ≤7

Step 1 — Review context

Check intern's most recent call status and Risk Status · Engagement Status · supervisor submission rate. Act immediately — do not wait for intern's WFL.

Step 2 — Email supervisor (Wed)

"Hi [Name], I'm checking in on [intern name]'s placement — I like to stay close to how things are going from your side. Could you give me a quick update on how they're getting on?"

Choose 2–3 questions:

  1. How would you describe [intern]'s progress this week?
  2. Is there anything about their work, attitude, or communication you'd want to flag?
  3. Are they getting enough structure and direction from your side?
  4. Is there anything you need from us to make this placement work better?

No response within 48h → follow up once more. Persistent non-response → SLA tagging.

Simultaneous Outreach

Contact both sides independently in the same week only when:

  1. Intern's account directly implicates supervisor (no tasks, no response, no feedback).
  2. Supervisor's account directly implicates intern (non-attendance, non-communication, non-delivery).
  3. Both scores ≤7 independently.
  4. Week 4 low score follows Week 3 At Risk or Critical.
ℹ️
Contact each side separately. Never share one side's verbatim feedback with the other.

🟢 Week 3: Trajectory Check Proactive · Every intern · Any day
Guiding Principle
This isn't a check-in. It's a trajectory assessment.
🟢
Trigger: Proactive — every intern, every Week 3. Pull the Week 1 call log before dialling — the Week 1 status is your entry point. 2 attempts + voicemail + text + email before declaring Unreachable.

Call Script

Opening
"Hi [Name], [PSA name] here. You're three weeks in, which is when I check in properly with everyone — not because anything's wrong, but because this is the point where it's a good time to get a read on how it's going. Is now a good time?"

Category 1 — Work Output mandatory

"Walk me through your last working day, start to finish — what did you actually do?"
Listen for: concrete activity. Vague accounts or "waiting" are output risk signals regardless of hours logged.
→ Account is thin: "Tell me about something you've worked on so far that you're genuinely proud of, or that you've enjoyed."
→ Unclear output: "When you finish a task, what happens to it — does your supervisor review it, use it, give you feedback?"

Category 2 — Hours Trajectory mandatory · ask every call

"Looking at your hours, you're at [X] against a target of [Y]. Talk me through what your week actually looks like — is that pace sustainable to the end?"
→ Gap exists: "What's getting in the way — task availability, your schedule, or something with your supervisor?"
→ Host-side issue: "Your first move is raising it with your host — let's word that message together now. And while that lands, start CareerBridge today so the hours keep counting."

Category 3 — Host Relationship mandatory

"How do you think your supervisor would describe your work so far?"
"I honestly don't know" = limited feedback signal. A confident, specific answer = healthy.
→ Relationship unclear: "When did you last get feedback from them — and what did they say?"
→ Surface avoidance: "Is there a conversation with your host you've been putting off?"
Trajectory Projection
"Here's the question I ask everyone at this point: if nothing changes between now and the end, how do you think this internship finishes? And what would you want to be different?"
Let them answer fully before responding.
Close
"So, in your own words: what's the one thing you're going to do before [specific date]?"
"I'm noting that down, and I'll check in on it."
⚠️ If resolution requires more than one agreed action, end the call and follow up asynchronously. Do not solve on the call.

🔵 Week 5A: Finish Strong Track A · 6–8 week programmes
Guiding Principle
Ten working days left. The plan is what the call produces.
🔵
Applies to: 6–8 week programmes entering the final stretch. Pull Week 3 log before dialling — it's your entry point, not a historical note. If Week 3 was At Risk or Critical, apply a higher bar: corroborating evidence required before scoring 4 or 5 in any category.

Call Script

Opening
"Hi [Name], [PSA name] here. You're in the home straight — about ten working days left — so this call is about making sure you finish strong and nothing trips you up at the end. Is now a good time?"

Category 1 — Hours to Target mandatory

"You've logged [X] hours, you need [Y] more, and you've got [submissions/assessments] outstanding. Walk me through your plan for getting those done in the time left."
→ Large gap: "What could realistically get in the way of that plan? And what do we do if it happens?"
→ Can't meet hours through host alone: "CareerBridge can help close part of this — let's choose the resources now so the hours count."

Category 2 — Execution Plan mandatory

"Is there anything still unresolved with your host — unclear final deliverables, handover work, anything you've been putting off — that we should sort this week rather than leaving it to the end?"
→ Loose ends: "What's your first move on that — and when will you make it?"

Category 3 — Intern Ownership mandatory

"Beyond just completing: what do you want your host's final feedback to say about you? What would you need to do in these last two weeks for them to say it?"
→ Passive intern: "If your supervisor had to describe your initiative in the last two weeks in one word, what do you think they'd say — and is that what you want?"
Close
"So by [Friday date] you'll have logged [n] hours and submitted [items] — that's the plan in your words. I'll be watching the Friday report and will check in if anything slips."

🔵 Week 5B: Midpoint Course-Correct Track B · 8+ week programmes
Guiding Principle
The second half should look different from the first. If it won't — change it today.
🔵
Applies to: 8+ week programmes at midpoint. Pull Week 3 log before dialling — the Week 3 commitment is the first question on this call, not a formality. If Week 3 was At Risk or Critical, apply a higher bar.

Call Script

Opening
"Hi [Name], [PSA name] here. You're roughly halfway through, which is the best point to take stock — enough time behind you to be honest about how it's going, and enough time ahead to change it. Is now a good time?"

Category 1 — Hours to Target mandatory

"You're at [X] hours against [Y] expected by now. At this pace you finish at [projection]. Talk me through it — and let's decide what needs to change this week, not later."
State the projection clearly. Do not soften the number.
→ Trajectory off: "Is this a scheduling issue, a task availability issue, or something else? Which can you fix yourself this week?"

Category 2 — Follow-Through mandatory

"Last time we spoke you committed to [commitment from Week 3 log]. How did that go?"
Completed: acknowledge clearly. Not completed: "what got in the way?" Do not move past this lightly.
→ Not completed: "Was it that you couldn't do it, or that you chose not to prioritise it? I'm asking because the answer changes what we do next."

Category 3 — Execution Plan mandatory

"Here's the big one: if the second half of this internship looked exactly like the first half, would you be satisfied at the end? Why, or why not?"
Let them answer fully. The quality of the answer tells you whether they have a plan or are drifting.
→ Make it concrete: "So let's design the second half: what's the one change that would make the biggest difference — and what's the first step you'll take before [date]?"
→ Supervisor check: "Is there a conversation you've been avoiding with your host — about more responsibility, clearer feedback, or different work?"
Evidence Check
"What's the strongest piece of work you can point to so far? And what do you want to be able to point to by the end that doesn't exist yet?"
Close

🔴 Escalation & Unreachable
Guiding Principle
If it's a risk, escalate. If it's a decision, own it.

Unreachable Protocol

🚫
Unreachable threshold: 2 call attempts with no answer · 1 voicemail left · 1 text with no response · 1 email with no response — all within the same week.
  1. Log all contact attempts with timestamps in HubSpot immediately.
  2. Flag to PAM via HubSpot task — do not wait for end of week.
  3. Contact the supervisor to confirm whether the intern is attending and engaging.
  4. Apply the Unreachable outcome from the relevant week's rubric.

SLA tagging applies. An intern who remains unresponsive after PAM visibility is raised is subject to SLA tagging per the Reliability & SLA Tagging Framework.

Ticket Responses

  • SLA: respond within 48 hours — maximum, not target.
  • Non-attendance, complete communication breakdown, safeguarding concerns → actioned same day.
  • If a ticket surfaces a risk equivalent to ≤7 score → treat as score-triggered follow-up.
  • Ticket from intern implicating supervisor → contact supervisor same week; do not wait for their WFL.

The aim is not to keep every internship running at all costs. The aim is to identify issues early, have the right conversations, and make the right decision.

Your Queue is your day. Open it first. Work it in order.

Open Cowork (the Claude desktop app). Select your name when prompted — your caseload loads automatically. It pulls live data from Metabase and HubSpot. No manual scanning.

🔴 CriticalIntern rated 5 or belowContact intern same day. Card shows both intern and supervisor scores.
🟡 At RiskIntern rated 6 or 7Contact intern Mon/Tue. Card shows both scores.
🏢 Supervisor ActionsHC Disengaged/Slipping, or supervisor assessments outstandingContact supervisor this week (Wed).

Key Features

  • Stat bar: Caseload · Critical · At Risk · Disengaged · On Track · Sup Actions · Assessments — all clickable to filter.
  • Intern cards: Engagement scores, hours, submission status, and a smart action line. Low intern scores (≤7) show a CareerBridge lesson suggestion inline.
  • Supervisor Actions section: Grouped by host company — one card per supervisor, with all their interns underneath.
  • Call tiles (Wk 1 / Wk 3 / Wk 5): Click to open the full script, rubric, and log template.
  • CareerBridge Reference panel: Expand below the search bar for full learner profiles (Part A) and problem scenarios (Part B) with copy-ready scripts.

Activating CareerBridge

The PSA sends the intern a message naming the specific lesson codes for their situation, confirms the hours count toward their target, and confirms they know how to access them on the platform. No platform-side setup required from the PSA — CareerBridge is self-serve. Confirmation happens through standard monitoring of the intern's next WFL submission, not a separate check-in.

👋 Onboarding
Guiding Principle
Five days to caseload-ready. If it's not in HubSpot, it didn't happen.
🌟
Your 5-day onboarding programme runs sequentially: shadow, score, log, triage, solo call. Work through it in order — it takes you from zero to running your own caseload. Once through it, this wiki is your day-to-day reference.

By the End of Your First Week

  • You know the model. The five-week cadence, triggers, and five non-negotiables — well enough to explain why a thriving intern with a silent supervisor is still a risk.
  • You run the tools. The Queue opens your day, HubSpot holds everything you do, Metabase tells you why a placement surfaced. No tool is a mystery.
  • You've proven it on real calls. Calibrated within ±2 of Chika's scoring, run your first scored call with your mentor listening, and triaged your own caseload — both sides, supervisors included.

Five Non-Negotiables Before Your First Call

  1. Every intern gets three proactive calls — Weeks 1, 3, and 5 — regardless of WFL scores. Programme requirement, not optional.
  2. A check-in is only complete when there is two-way communication. Sending a message does not count.
  3. Every call ends with the intern stating one specific commitment in their own words. Log it verbatim and follow up on it.
  4. If it is not in HubSpot, it did not happen.
  5. Your judgment matters. The SOPs are your starting point, not your ceiling.
